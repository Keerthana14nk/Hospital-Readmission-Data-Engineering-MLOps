ABSTRACT

Hospital readmissions within 30 days of inpatient discharge impose substantial clinical vulnerabilities on patients and trigger severe financial penalties for healthcare systems under the Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program (HRRP) administered by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). However, raw Electronic Health Record (EHR) and clinical encounter data are characterized by high-dimensional complexity, non-standardized missing tokens, class imbalance, and strict patient privacy regulations. This project, titled “Hospital Readmission Analytics and 30-Day Prediction,” implements an end-to-end, privacy-preserving data engineering and production MLOps pipeline designed to ingest, process, warehouse, analyze, predict, and govern 30-day hospital readmissions.

The system utilizes the retrospective UCI Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset, representing 101,766 inpatient encounters across 130 medical centers over a 10-year period with 50 clinical and demographic attributes. A Python-based automated extraction and transformation pipeline performs missing-value harmonization, clinical ICD-9 diagnosis grouping into 8 disease categories, clinical feature aggregation, and cryptographic surrogate key de-identification using SHA-256 hashing to enforce HIPAA-grade privacy. An automated data quality validation gate enforces four integrity constraints, staging verified records into a PostgreSQL dimensional data warehouse comprising fact and summary tables while isolating anomalies in an operational error quarantine.

An interactive clinical analytics platform built with Streamlit visualizes executive healthcare metrics, readmission correlations across demographic brackets, and high-risk patient cohorts. The MLOps pipeline extension models 30-day readmission as a cost-sensitive binary classification task to mitigate the acute 1:8 class imbalance (11.16% readmission rate). Three machine learning architectures—Logistic Regression with balanced weighting, Random Forest with balanced subsampling, and XGBoost with positive class weighting—are benchmarked with MLflow experiment tracking. XGBoost is established as the champion model with an ROC-AUC of 0.6815 and a recall of 60.55%, successfully passing an automated operational deployment gate.

The champion pipeline is serialized and served through a low-latency FastAPI REST service offering real-time bedside risk scoring, probability gauge estimation, and targeted clinical intervention recommendations, packaged via Docker for reproducible deployment. Continuous governance is maintained through statistical drift monitoring using Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests and Population Stability Index (PSI). The integrated workflow bridges robust data engineering with responsible healthcare machine learning to deliver clinical decision support.

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 Background

The modernization of healthcare infrastructure and the widespread adoption of Electronic Health Record (EHR) systems have produced extensive repositories of clinical, demographic, and administrative transaction data. Hospital encounters record crucial information regarding patient admission pathways, length of stay, laboratory diagnostic intensity, surgical and therapeutic procedures, pharmacotherapy regimens, and post-discharge outcomes.

Among clinical quality measures, 30-day all-cause hospital readmission serves as a paramount benchmark of healthcare quality, transition-of-care effectiveness, and patient safety. Under the Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program (HRRP) established by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), medical centers with readmission rates exceeding national thresholds face severe financial penalties alongside compromised clinical reputations. Identifying patients at elevated risk of rapid readmission prior to discharge enables healthcare institutions to proactively implement targeted clinical transition bundles, such as multidisciplinary discharge medication reconciliation, 48-hour follow-up telephone triage, and expedited outpatient clinic scheduling.

However, the practical utility of clinical datasets depends on data engineering rigour and governance. Retrospective clinical records routinely exhibit missing tokens, duplicate observations, severe class imbalances (where readmission occurs in only a small minority of encounters), and stringent regulatory constraints regarding Protected Health Information (PHI). Data engineering provides the structured methodology to ingest, normalize, de-identify, validate, and warehouse clinical data. Machine learning and MLOps build upon this foundation to automate predictive risk scoring, enforce quality gates, and monitor model performance over time.

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT AND OBJECTIVES

2.1 Problem Statement

Clinical datasets derived from hospital encounters contain valuable information regarding patient demographics, admission pathways, clinical diagnoses, length of stay, procedure counts, and pharmacotherapy regimens. However, direct analytical exploitation of raw clinical data presents significant technical and operational hurdles:

  • Data Inconsistency and Quality Deficits: Raw hospital datasets frequently contain inconsistent missing value tokens, duplicate entries, and out-of-bounds metrics that distort analytical totals and downstream predictions.
  • Patient Privacy and De-Identification: Healthcare analytics must adhere to strict regulatory privacy mandates (such as HIPAA). Analyzing direct patient identifiers exposes institutions to severe legal vulnerabilities.
  • Target Leakage and Artificial Accuracy: Retrospective datasets often contain discharge or outcome fields that artificially leak the target class, causing machine learning models to appear accurate in training while failing completely in live deployment.
  • Acute Class Imbalance: 30-day hospital readmissions represent a minority clinical event (typically 10%–15%). Standard machine learning models trained on imbalanced data optimize overall accuracy by predicting zero readmissions for 100% of cases, missing every deteriorating patient.
  • Lack of Operational Lifecycle Governance: Data pipelines and machine learning models are often executed as isolated scripts without centralized orchestration, experiment tracking, validation gates, or drift detection.

The problem addressed by this project is the engineering of an integrated, automated, and privacy-preserving system that extracts raw hospital encounter records, validates and warehouses them in a PostgreSQL data mart, delivers clinical insights via an interactive dashboard, and implements a governed MLOps pipeline for 30-day hospital readmission risk prediction.

2.2 Objectives

The primary technical and clinical objectives of the project are:

  • To extract 101,766 hospital encounter records from the UCI Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset.
  • To standardize missing tokens, normalize schema representations, and group ICD-9 diagnosis codes.
  • To enforce HIPAA-compliant de-identification by generating SHA-256 cryptographic surrogate patient keys.
  • To implement an automated data quality gate that routes invalid records to an error quarantine.
  • To design and populate a PostgreSQL dimensional data warehouse with fact and summary marts.
  • To build an interactive clinical analytics dashboard in Streamlit for population health exploration.
  • To engineer clinical predictors while strictly eliminating target leakage and surrogate identifiers.
  • To train and benchmark Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost using class-weighted cost functions.
  • To track machine learning experiments, log parameters and evaluation artifacts, and register models with MLflow.
  • To enforce an automated operational validation gate asserting minimum clinical discrimination and recall.
  • To deploy the champion model as a real-time REST endpoint using FastAPI with Docker containerization.
  • To establish post-deployment statistical drift monitoring using Kolmogorov-Smirnov and PSI metrics.

The attribute `encounter_id` serves as the primary key for the admission fact table, ensuring that each hospitalization is uniquely identifiable. The raw medical record identifier (`patient_nbr`) was permanently expunged during transformation and replaced with `patient_key`, an irreversible 16-character SHA-256 cryptographic surrogate hash (`SHA-256("patient:" + patient_nbr)[:16]`). Clinical utilization metrics (`num_lab_procedures`, `num_medications`, `time_in_hospital`, and `prior_admission_count`) quantify patient acuity and polypharmacy load. To prevent target leakage, all retrospective indicators and identifier hashes are strictly removed from feature matrices prior to model training.

5. DATA INGESTION AND ETL PIPELINE

5.1 ETL Workflow

The ETL (Extract, Transform, Load) pipeline converts raw, heterogeneous healthcare records into validated, privacy-safe, and analytically structured data warehouse entities. The pipeline is executed in five discrete steps:

  • Step 1: Extract: Programmatically pulls the 101,766 encounter records from the UCI repository or local archive and deposits an unedited copy into `data/raw/diabetic_data.csv`, accompanied by `ingestion_metadata.json`.
  • Step 2: Transform: Normalizes inconsistent tokens, categorizes ICD-9 codes, generates cryptographic patient surrogate keys, derives clinical features, and standardizes column representations.
  • Step 3: Quality Validation: Runs automated pre-load validation rules. Rows meeting all criteria pass to `data/cleaned/encounters_cleaned.csv`; failed rows are quarantined in `data/errors/rejected_records.csv`.
  • Step 4: Load: Executes schema initialization (`sql/schema.sql`) and loads valid encounters into PostgreSQL (`mart.admission_fact`), simultaneously populating patient, diagnosis, and readmission summary marts.
  • Step 5: Pipeline Tracking: Records execution timestamps, total row counts, and status in the operations audit table (`ops.pipeline_run`).

5.2 Data Cleaning and Privacy-Preserving Transformation

Data cleaning and privacy transformations are executed by `src/transform/clean.py` and encompass four critical operations:

  • Missing Value Harmonization: Replaced arbitrary missing value indicators (`?`, `Unknown/Invalid`, `Not Available`, whitespace) with standardized `pd.NA` tokens. Columns with extreme missingness (>80% missing, such as `weight`) were flagged.
  • Encounter Deduplication: Verified encounter uniqueness on `encounter_id` (101,766 unique encounters).
  • HIPAA De-Identification: To eliminate patient re-identification risk while retaining cross-visit linkage for longitudinal patient analytics, raw medical record numbers were converted to irreversible SHA-256 surrogate hashes:
    patient_key = SHA-256("patient:" + patient_nbr)[:16]
    The raw `patient_nbr` column was permanently purged from all processed outputs.
  • ICD-9 Diagnosis Clinical Grouping: Clinical diagnosis codes (`diag_1`, `diag_2`, `diag_3`) contain thousands of granular codes. The primary diagnosis (`diag_1`) was mapped into 8 clinically recognized disease families: Circulatory (390–459, 785), Respiratory (460–519, 786), Digestive (520–579, 787), Diabetes (250.xx), Genitourinary (580–629, 788), Neoplasms (140–239), Injury (800–999), and Other (all remaining codes).
  • Feature Engineering: Constructed `prior_admission_count` by summing prior inpatient, outpatient, and emergency room visits; established continuous `length_of_stay`; and generated the exact binary target `readmitted_within_30_days` (1 if `<30`, else 0).

5.3 Pipeline Orchestration

The workflow is governed by Apache Airflow through DAG `hospital_readmission_mlops_pipeline`. Airflow enforces task dependencies, captures detailed logs, manages retries, and coordinates the sequential execution of extraction, transformation, quality gates, and database loading.

14

5.4 Error Handling and Logging

Reliable healthcare pipelines must enforce strict fault tolerance, comprehensive audit logging, and automated error containment. In clinical systems, silently dropping corrupted records or ignoring data anomalies can lead to biased analyses and dangerous clinical models.

The pipeline implements a three-tier error management and audit logging strategy:

  • Operational Quarantine (`ops.rejected_records`): Rather than silently discarding invalid encounters, the validation module (`src/quality/validate.py`) segregates every violating record into `data/errors/rejected_records.csv` and loads them into PostgreSQL table `ops.rejected_records`. Each rejected record is tagged with the specific validation rule that triggered rejection, the failure timestamp, and the raw payload. This preserves an auditable historical trail for clinical data governance.
  • Pipeline Run Audit Logging (`ops.pipeline_run`): Every invocation of the ETL and loading pipeline inserts an audit row into `ops.pipeline_run`. The log captures:
    • `run_id`: Unique execution identifier.
    • `execution_start` and `execution_end`: High-precision UTC timestamps.
    • `stage`: The executing pipeline component (e.g., Ingestion, Transformation, DW Load).
    • `rows_processed`: Total number of records read from upstream stages.
    • `rows_inserted`: Total valid records inserted into warehouse fact tables.
    • `rows_rejected`: Total invalid records redirected to quarantine.
    • `status`: Terminal state (`SUCCESS`, `FAILED`, or `WARNING`).
  • Task-Level Orchestration Logging: Apache Airflow maintains persistent, isolated execution logs for every DAG task instance. If a task encounters a database timeout, missing source file, or memory bottleneck, Airflow captures the exact traceback, halts downstream dependent tasks, logs the alert, and supports automated retries with exponential backoff.

This rigorous logging and containment framework ensures complete traceability from the raw UCI CSV download to the final PostgreSQL fact table, providing clinicians and auditors with verifiable confidence in data integrity.

6.3 Data Quality Checks

Data quality validation is enforced by `src/quality/validate.py` prior to database loading. The module applies four strict validation assertions:

  • Rule 1 (`encounter_id_not_null`): Asserts that `encounter_id` is non-null and numeric. Primary key integrity is essential for relational consistency.
  • Rule 2 (`patient_key_not_null`): Asserts that the SHA-256 hashed surrogate key is non-null, ensuring 100% de-identification compliance.
  • Rule 3 (`length_of_stay_between_1_and_14`): Asserts that inpatient length of stay falls within the clinically valid range of 1 to 14 days. Encounters outside this boundary represent erroneous data.
  • Rule 4 (`readmission_class_valid`): Asserts that the readmission class belongs strictly to `['<30', '>30', 'NO']`.

Across the 101,766 raw records processed, 101,766 records (100.0%) satisfied all four quality constraints, confirming the structural integrity of the curated cohort. The validation gate logged a success status and approved the batch for warehouse insertion.

6.4 Data Mart Design

Three analytical data marts were constructed to optimize clinical dashboard responsiveness:

  • `mart.diagnosis_summary`: Aggregates encounter volume, average length of stay, and 30-day readmission percentage grouped by the 8 ICD-9 clinical families.
  • `mart.patient_summary`: Rollup of total encounters, cumulative days in hospital, and prior admissions grouped by `patient_key`.
  • `mart.readmission_summary`: Multi-dimensional cross-tabulation of readmission rates across age brackets and admission categories.

8. MLOPS PIPELINE EXTENSION

8.1 30-Day Readmission Definition and Class Imbalance

In accordance with CMS HRRP definitions, 30-day hospital readmission is formulated as a binary classification problem: predicting whether an inpatient encounter will result in an unplanned readmission within exactly 30 days of discharge (`readmitted_within_30_days = 1`), versus readmissions beyond 30 days or no readmission (`readmitted_within_30_days = 0`).

Across the 101,766 verified encounters, exactly 11,357 encounters (11.16%) experienced readmission within 30 days, while 90,409 encounters (88.84%) did not. This represents an acute 1:8 class imbalance. Standard unweighted algorithms optimize raw accuracy by predicting class 0 for every patient, producing an apparently high accuracy of 88.84% while achieving a recall of 0.00%. In healthcare, missing 100% of deteriorating patients is completely unacceptable. Mitigating this imbalance through cost-sensitive class weighting is therefore a core architectural requirement.

8.2 Clinical Feature Engineering

To prevent artificial inflation of predictive accuracy, all target-correlated columns (`readmitted`, `readmission_class`, `readmitted_within_30_days`), redundant metrics (`age_years_midpoint`), and identifiers (`encounter_id`, `patient_key`) were strictly eliminated. 8.3 Model Development

Three distinct algorithms were implemented and trained using an 80/20 stratified split (81,412 training encounters, 20,354 holdout test encounters):

  • Logistic Regression (Balanced): Linear baseline with L2 regularization and `class_weight='balanced'`.
  • Random Forest (Balanced): Bagging ensemble of 100 decision trees with `class_weight='balanced_subsample'` and `max_depth=12`.
  • XGBoost (Scale Pos Weight): Gradient-boosted tree ensemble with `scale_pos_weight=7.96` (ratio of negative to positive training cases) and `max_depth=5`.
20

Model performance is evaluated on the holdout test set using clinically relevant classification metrics:

  • Recall (Sensitivity): The proportion of actual 30-day readmissions correctly identified. In hospital transition planning, maximizing recall ensures that high-risk patients are not discharged without follow-up care.
  • Precision: The proportion of predicted readmission alerts that represent true readmissions.
  • ROC-AUC: The Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve, quantifying discrimination power across all classification thresholds independent of decision cutoffs.
  • PR-AUC: Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve, providing a robust metric for imbalanced clinical cohorts.

Input Validation and Clinical Risk Stratification

The request payload is strictly validated via Pydantic schema `PatientEncounterInput`. The service derives engineered aggregations (`length_of_stay`, `prior_admission_count`), passes features to the champion pipeline, and returns:

  • `prediction`: Binary classification (1 = Readmitted <30 days, 0 = No).
  • `readmission_probability`: Precise calibrated risk score (0.00 to 1.00).
  • `risk_category`: Clinically actionable tier: Low (<20%), Moderate (20%–35%), or High (≥35%).
  • `recommended_action`: Specific transition protocol (e.g., standard discharge vs. mandatory 48-hour follow-up telephone call and medication reconciliation).

Docker Containerization: The API application is containerized via `Dockerfile.api`. Exposing port 8000, the container packages Python 3.12, serialized model artifacts, and all runtime dependencies, guaranteeing identical execution across development, staging, and cloud environments.

8.6 Quality Gates, Monitoring and Retraining

In high-stakes clinical deployments, maintaining strict model governance is essential. Patient demographics, seasonal epidemics, and evolving physician prescribing patterns inevitably cause feature distributions to diverge from training baselines over time (data drift and concept drift).

1. Automated Operational Quality Gate (`ml/validate_model_gate.py`)

Before any newly trained model can be promoted to the production model registry or served by the API, an automated quality gate enforces strict acceptance criteria:

  • Asserts that holdout test ROC-AUC ≥ 0.60 (Passed: 0.6815).
  • Asserts that minority class Recall ≥ 40.0% (Passed: 60.55%).
  • Verifies serialized pipeline artifact integrity (`models/champion_model.pkl`).

If any assertion fails, the pipeline aborts immediately with a non-zero exit code, raising an alert in Airflow and preventing substandard models from ever reaching patient-facing serving layers. The signoff report is exported to `models/validation_gate_result.json`.

2. Statistical Distribution Drift Monitoring (`ml/monitor_drift.py`)

Post-deployment inference batches are evaluated against baseline training distributions using rigorous statistical criteria:

  • Continuous Variables: Two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test (`ks_2samp`) and Population Stability Index (PSI) across numeric features (`num_lab_procedures`, `num_medications`, `time_in_hospital`, `prior_admission_count`).
  • Categorical Variables: Chi-square test of independence comparing demographic and diagnosis group frequency shifts.
  • Operational Action Thresholds:
    • PSI < 0.10: Stable distribution; normal operation maintained.
    • 0.10 ≤ PSI < 0.25: Moderate distribution shift; operational warning logged.
    • PSI ≥ 0.25: Significant drift detected; automated retraining pipeline triggered.

Drift results are compiled into `models/drift_report.json` and rendered live within the Streamlit model governance tab, ensuring transparent operational oversight.

Clinical Decision Threshold Optimization

In hospital transition planning, the operational cost of a False Negative (discharging an at-risk patient who subsequently suffers an emergency readmission or fatal complication) far exceeds the cost of a False Positive (assigning a nurse follow-up phone call to a patient who would have remained stable).

By sweeping the decision cutoff threshold on the champion XGBoost model, clinical departments can adjust operating points according to hospital staffing capacity:

  • Cutoff = 0.15: Sensitivity climbs to 83.1% with 14.2% precision. Recommended for high-sensitivity emergency transition triage where missing any vulnerable patient carries severe risk.
  • Cutoff = 0.20 (Recommended Baseline): Achieves balanced performance with 60.6% Recall and 17.9% Precision. Optimal for hospital-wide discharge medication reconciliation.
  • Cutoff = 0.30: Sensitivity is 38.2% with 21.8% precision. Suitable when intensive home-nurse visitation resources are severely constrained.

Privacy Preservation and Regulatory Compliance

The project rigorously enforced HIPAA de-identification through SHA-256 surrogate key hashing. Unlike reversible encryption schemes that require secure key management, the deterministic surrogate hash permanently eliminates direct medical record numbers while preserving relational aggregation capabilities across multi-visit patients.

Production Operational Readiness

The integration of FastAPI, MLflow, and Docker transforms an academic machine learning experiment into an operational microservice. The API returns real-time risk predictions in under 35 milliseconds, enabling direct embedding into bedside electronic health record software or nursing triage tablets.

10. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE

10.1 Conclusion

The project “Hospital Readmission Analytics and 30-Day Prediction” demonstrates the successful end-to-end implementation of an integrated healthcare data engineering and production MLOps pipeline using 101,766 inpatient encounters from the UCI Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset.

The data engineering component implemented automated ingestion, missing token harmonization, ICD-9 diagnosis categorization into 8 disease families, HIPAA-compliant SHA-256 cryptographic surrogate key de-identification, automated 4-rule data quality validation, and PostgreSQL dimensional warehousing (`mart.admission_fact`). Apache Airflow scheduled and orchestrated all pipeline stages with complete fault tolerance. The interactive Streamlit dashboard delivered executive clinical analytics, demographic readmission trends, and high-risk cohort filtering.

The MLOps pipeline extension formulated 30-day readmission as a cost-sensitive classification problem to address the acute 1:8 class imbalance. Through MLflow experiment tracking, three models were benchmarked, with class-weighted XGBoost emerging as the champion (ROC-AUC 0.6815, Recall 60.55%). The model successfully cleared an automated operational deployment gate and was deployed via a low-latency FastAPI REST service packaged in Docker. Post-deployment statistical drift monitoring using Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests and PSI ensures long-term operational governance.

10.2 Limitations

Several academic and technical limitations should be acknowledged:

  • Retrospective Data Scope: The dataset captures encounters between 1999 and 2008. Contemporary pharmacological therapies (e.g., SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 agonists) are not represented.
  • Absence of Post-Discharge Determinants: Crucial social determinants of health (SDOH), such as patient income, transportation access, outpatient medication adherence, and caregiver support, are absent from the dataset but strongly influence real-world readmission.
  • Broad Clinical Grouping: Aggregating thousands of granular ICD-9 diagnosis codes into 8 broad disease families sacrifices sub-disease specificity in favour of model parsimony.
  • Static Retraining Pipeline: While distribution drift detection is fully functional, automated end-to-end retraining currently requires manual supervisory approval.

10.3 Future Scope

The project provides a foundation that can be extended across several clinical directions:

  • Real-Time FHIR/HL7 Streaming: Integrate Apache Kafka with Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR) protocols to ingest continuous bedside vital sign telemetry.
  • Clinical NLP on Physician Notes: Incorporate BioClinicalBERT to extract unstructured sentiment and clinical acuity from discharge summaries and nursing progress notes.
28

Future Scope (Continued)

  • Federated Multi-Hospital Learning: Extend model training across decentralized hospital consortia using federated learning protocols (e.g., PySyft or Flower), training globally optimal models without centralizing sensitive patient data.
  • Reinforcement Learning for Transition Care: Implement contextual bandits to recommend personalized discharge care bundles (e.g., optimal timing of home nurse visits) to maximize clinical recovery while minimizing hospital costs.
  • Automated CI/CD Retraining Webhooks: Configure GitHub Actions and Airflow webhooks that automatically trigger model retraining, validation gating, and blue-green Docker container updates whenever PSI drift exceeds 0.25.
  • Explainable Bedside AI (SHAP / LIME): Integrate real-time SHAP waterfall charts into the FastAPI response payload, providing attending physicians with patient-specific feature attribution explanations for every risk score.
Final Summary

This project successfully establishes a comprehensive, HIPAA-conscious, and production-ready data engineering and MLOps architecture for 30-day hospital readmission analytics. By harmonizing robust data warehouse principles, automated Airflow orchestration, class-imbalance mitigation, MLflow lifecycle tracking, and containerized FastAPI serving, the system provides a scalable technical blueprint for transforming retrospective clinical encounter data into actionable, life-saving bedside clinical decision support.
